In the
Pocket.

There’s a word bands use — the pocket. Here’s the easy meaning: playing right in step with the drummer. Not rushing ahead, not dragging behind. When we’re all in the pocket, the music stops feeling like a scramble and starts to feel good — like it’s carrying us.

00 · Where We Left Off

Last Time
We Made a Rule.

In Vol 02 we learned about dynamics — loud, soft, and space — and we made one golden rule: get bigger by playing more, never by playing faster. We said the beat is like a fence post stuck in the ground. Straight and still. It does not move.

Good. That rule tells you not to speed up. But it doesn’t yet tell you how to stay glued to that fence post — how to actually sit right on the beat, together, all night. That’s this week.

The place where you’re locked to the beat, not early and not late, has a name. Bands call it the pocket. Learn to live in it and half our rushing problems disappear on their own.

01 · The Big Idea

It’s Just
Walking Together.

Picture walking down the road next to a friend, in step — left, right, left, right — without even trying. That easy, matched feeling is the pocket. Now picture one of you speeding up. Suddenly you’re bumping shoulders, out of step, and it feels awkward. That’s rushing.

Playing in a band is exactly that. The beat is the footstep you all share. When everyone puts their note down on the same step, at the same moment, the music feels solid and easy. When one person keeps jumping ahead, the whole thing trips.

The Bible actually pins the whole thing on one plain question:

Amos 3 · 3

"Do two walk together, unless they have agreed?"

Walking together means agreeing on the step · WEB translation

Two people can’t walk together unless they’ve agreed — agreed on the pace, agreed on the step. That’s us. Before we worry about clever parts or big choruses, we have to agree on the step. And the step is the beat. Remember what we said in Vol 02 — the goal is to play as one. Well, this is where “as one” actually happens or falls apart: in the timing.

02 · Three Places to Land

Rush, Drag,
& the Pocket.

Every time you play a note, it lands in one of three places. A tiny bit early, a tiny bit late, or right on the beat. Same three places, every single time. So it’s worth learning what each one feels like.

01
Rushing
▸ Landing early
Your note jumps in front of the beat. It feels panicky, like you’re dragging everyone faster. This is the most common mistake in the room — and it usually happens the moment a song gets exciting.
02
Dragging
▸ Landing late
Your note flops in behind the beat. It feels heavy and tired, like the song is wading through mud. Less common than rushing, but it kills the energy just as fast.
03
The Pocket
▸ Right on the beat
Your note lands with the drum. Not early, not late — together. This is the one we want. It feels relaxed and strong at the same time, and the whole band suddenly sounds twice as tight.

Reading about it only gets you so far — timing is a sound, so let’s hear it. Tap play below. The grey dot is the drum, keeping steady time. The red dot is you. Switch between the three and listen to where your note lands.

03 · Who Holds the Clock

Follow
the Drummer.

If everyone tries to keep their own time, we get a room full of clocks all ticking slightly differently — chaos. So we don’t all keep time. We pick one clock, and everybody sets themselves to it. That clock is the drummer.

BJ on the drums holds the time for the whole band. His job is to be the steady one. Your job is to listen down to him and lock on — not to lead him, not to pull him faster, just to sit on what he’s giving you.

Two specials things to lock onto:

→ The kick drum (the low “boom”) and the snare (the sharp “crack” on beats 2 and 4). If your note lands with the kick and snare, you’re in the pocket. That’s your target.

And bass — this one’s for you especially: marry the kick drum. When the kick goes boom, your bass note goes with it. Kick and bass together are the engine of the whole song. Get those two locked and everything on top has a floor to stand on.

1 Corinthians 14 · 40

"Let all things be done decently and in order."

One clock, everyone in order · WEB translation

Order isn’t a boring church word. It’s the thing that lets ten people make one sound instead of ten. When we all agree to follow the same clock, that’s order — and order is what makes room for God to move without us tripping over each other.

"The drummer keeps time. Everyone else keeps to the drummer."

▸ The one to remember · for Calvary Worship

04 · The Hard Part

Don’t Chase It.
Sit In It.

Here’s the honest bit. Rushing isn’t really a music problem — it’s a nerves problem. When a song lifts and the room gets going, your heart speeds up, and your hands want to speed up with it. Every band on earth fights this.

So the skill is almost the opposite of what feels natural. When the chorus hits and everything in you wants to push — breathe, sit back, and trust the beat. Let the drummer carry it. You don’t have to drive; you just have to stay. The pocket feels a tiny bit lazy, like you’re leaning back into a chair rather than leaning forward off the edge. That leaned-back feeling is exactly right.

1 Corinthians 15 · 58

"Be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the Lord’s work."

Steady and unmoved · WEB translation

Steadfast. Immovable. That’s a picture of the heart, and it’s also a picture of good timing. A player who won’t be rushed — who stays planted when the room is pulling — is a gift to the whole team. Being unmovable on the beat is a small way of being unmovable, full stop.

Psalm 40 · 2

"He set my feet on a rock, and gave my steps a firm place to stand."

A firm place to stand · WEB translation

That’s what a steady beat is for the band — a firm place to stand. When the time is solid, nobody’s slipping, everybody knows where the ground is, and we’re free to actually worship instead of fighting to stay together. Firm feet, firm time. Same idea.

05 · So Here’s the Job

Four Things
to Take to Sunday.

Hold These Four.

1. Walk together. Put your note on the same step as everyone else. In step is the whole game.

2. Follow the drummer. One clock. BJ keeps time; everyone keeps to BJ — lock to the kick and snare.

3. Bass marries the kick. Low boom and bass note together. That’s the engine.

4. Don’t chase — sit back. When it lifts, breathe and stay put. Leaned-back beats rushed.

If you only remember one, remember this: don’t chase the beat — sit in it. Get that one right and we already sound twice as tight.

"The drummer keeps time. Everyone else keeps to the drummer."

Let’s find the pocket and stay in it together. See you on the call.
