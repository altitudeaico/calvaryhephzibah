Bodies as Service

Prof. B Adebisi

One Sunday, a message for the week.How the sermon was shared in short videos and still posts, with every piece available to watch or read below.

What was preached

A simple invitation to serve.

On Sunday 15 November 2026, Prof. B Adebisi preached Bodies as Service from Romans 12:1. Sister Tash Campbell read Romans 12:1-18. His message, in his own words, was simple: "Let's not complicate it."

  1. You are bought at a price. Our worth comes from the price paid for us, the blood of Jesus Christ (1 Corinthians 6:19-20).
  2. You are the temple, the altar, the sacrifice and the priest. In Christ, the separate parts of Old Testament worship come together in us, so worship and service are open to every believer.
  3. Offer your body as a living sacrifice. "Bodies" means everything God has given us: talents, abilities, platform and gifts (Ephesians 4:7).
  4. Show up and do your own corner. Serving God is showing up with what you are good at, without comparing yourself with others.
  5. PHD: Pursue God, Hear Him, Do what He says. The wise and the foolish builders both heard; only one acted (Matthew 7:24-27).
  6. KTP: Knowledge, Talent, Practice. Talent is less than 20 percent; knowledge and practice make up the rest.
  7. Don't know your calling? Start serving. Serving helps clarify your calling, and we can ask God for help along the way.

Why we do this

So the message can travel.

A Sunday sermon is an hour at most. These posts carry the word through the rest of the week, with two aims.

Helping the church go deeper. Members meet the message again on their phones, are drawn back to rewatch the full sermon, and are encouraged to study it for themselves.

Reaching people beyond our walls. The same message, packaged for social media, reaches people in Manchester and around the world who may never have stepped into a church. Each post invites them to hear more, draw closer to God, and connect with Calvary.

Reels are 20 to 60 second moments from the sermon, edited with captions and on-screen text so they hold attention while people scroll. Each one ends by pointing to the full sermon on YouTube.

Still posts stop the scroll with a single line from the message. The caption then does the deeper work: a short story from the sermon, the scripture behind it, and one practical step to take that week.

Every caption is checked against the sermon itself, so nothing is said that the preacher did not say.

Have you ever felt you needed the right person or the right resources before you could do something for God? Prof. B Adebisi explained something interesting in Bodies as Service. Talent is only 20%

Read caption +
Being naturally good at something doesn't mean you can stop learning. Prof. B Adebisi shared a useful idea in Bodies as Service. He calls it KTP. Knowledge, Talent and Practice. He explained that natural talent accounts for less than twenty percent of how effective someone becomes. The rest comes from learning and practising. Think about the naturally brilliant friend who never studied and didn't get an A. Or someone who's naturally good at football or singing. Talent matters, but talent alone isn't enough. He encouraged people to study what makes others good at their work. If you sing, learn from excellent singers. If you're involved in media, listen to people who have done it well. There are resources on YouTube. Then practise what you learn. He described knowledge and practice as accounting for 80%. So if there's something you're good at, don't stop at being naturally gifted. Have you ever enjoyed doing something so much that being asked to do it felt like a gift? Prof. B Adebisi shared a personal experience in Bodies as Service. He was invited to speak at a church where young people had gathered, including university students and young couples. They spent the evening talking together. His flight that day was cancelled, which gave him even more time with them. Afterwards, the pastor wanted to give him a gift. But he declined. He explained that this was a personal conviction about what he believed God had told him. It wasn't a rule from the Bible that everybody needed to follow. His point was about recognising what you're called to do and wanting to do it. Think about the abilities you already have. Is there something you genuinely want to contribute, without anyone having to persuade you? Everywhere you step

Sun 22 Nov, 17:30 · UK

InstagramFacebookYouTube
Read caption +
Imagine having to travel a long distance just to reach the place where you could offer something to God. Prof. B Adebisi described how things worked in earlier times during Bodies as Service. People travelled to the temple in Jerusalem to bring their sacrifices. They needed a priest to offer them on the altar. And the journeys themselves could be long. He explained that people sang as they travelled. Some of those songs are recorded in Psalms 120 to 134, known as the Songs of Ascents. He also remembered working in Haifa and travelling in that part of the world. Imagine making those journeys without a car or sat nav, using the sun to find your way. Then he made the contrast. "You don't have to do that. Everywhere you step into, there is altar, there is priest, there is sacrifice, there is everything." His point was that serving God is no longer limited to travelling to one particular building. He said we can now go to people.
