One thing to be clear about. Early in the rehearsal we worked Lord We Proclaim You Now in G, after F sat too high for the top note. Later, when we set the keys for the whole service, we moved all the worship songs to A♭ and Esther confirmed that sat comfortably.

02 · How We Work

The Group Chat
Is Not Optional.

The rehearsal opened here, before a single note was played.

The notice went out on Tuesday. Almost everybody had read it. Almost nobody had replied.

The reason was fair enough. People were busy and meant to come back to it later. But the effect is the same either way. When nobody answers, the person planning the service has no idea who is coming, who is covering which part, or whether anything needs to change before the rehearsal.

So the standard we agreed is small. You do not need to write a paragraph. A thumbs up is enough. It takes two seconds and it tells the whole team you have seen it.

And if something is a problem, that is exactly the message worth sending. Better to say it on Tuesday than to find out on Sunday morning.

03 · Praise · Key of B

Four Songs
We Already Know.

Nothing new here. The job is tightening what we already play.

The praise set is Trading My Sorrows, Lord You Are Good, Friend of God and Every Praise, all in B.

We have played all four at Calvary before, so we are not rebuilding the arrangements. BJ is sending round links to old livestreams instead, so everyone can hear the version we actually play rather than the version on the record. They are not always the same, and turning up with the record in your head is how a song falls apart in the second chorus.

That is the homework. Listen back, find your part in it, and arrive already knowing where the song goes.

04 · Worship · Key of A♭

Three Songs,
One Journey.

Most of the rehearsal went here. These three run together with no stop in between, so treat them as one long song in three parts.

The shape repeats in all three, and it is worth naming plainly because once you have it, you can hold all three in your head at once:

Dr Caster starts on her own. The band comes in underneath. Esther and the rest join. Then it builds.

Nobody comes in at full volume. Everything grows.

05 · Offering & Close · A♭

Two Simple Ones
To Finish.

Both stay in A♭, so there is no key change to manage after worship.

Close
You Are Worthy. "King of kings, Lord of lords, You are worthy." An old one Calvary has sung for years. It needs no arrangement, only a clean start.

Keeping both in A♭ was a deliberate call rather than a coincidence. The fewer moves there are, the fewer places the service can wobble.

Take These To Sunday.

1. Praise is in B. Everything else is in A♭. One change in the whole service.

2. A♭, not G. If you learned it on Friday night, move it up a semitone.

3. Dr Caster starts alone. Every time. Wait for her, then come in underneath.

4. Come in soft and grow. Nothing starts at full volume.

5. Count the verses. Two solo, two together, then chorus.

6. Answer the chat. A thumbs up is enough.
