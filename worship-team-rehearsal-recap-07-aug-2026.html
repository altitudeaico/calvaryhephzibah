01 How We Work

The group chat
is not optional.

The rehearsal opened here, before a single note was played.

The rehearsal notice went out on Tuesday. Almost everybody had read it. Almost nobody had replied.

The explanation was fair enough: people were busy and meant to come back to it. But the effect is the same either way. When nobody answers, the person planning the service has no idea who is coming, who is covering which part, or whether anything needs changing before Thursday.

The standard we agreed is small. You do not need to write a paragraph. A thumbs up or a single emoji is enough. It takes two seconds and it tells the whole team you have seen it.

If something is a problem, that is exactly the message worth sending. Better to say it on Tuesday than to have it surface on Sunday morning.

The praise set is Trading My Sorrows, Lord You Are Good, Friend of God and Every Praise, all in B.

That is the homework for the praise set. Listen back, find your part in it, and turn up already knowing where the song goes.

03 Worship · Key of A♭

Three songs,
one journey.

Most of the rehearsal went here. These three run together with no stop between them.

The shape is the same each time, and it is worth naming it plainly because it repeats: Dr Caster starts alone, the band comes in underneath, Esther and the rest join, then it builds. Nobody comes in at full volume. Everything grows.

Lord We Proclaim You Now A♭
Also known as "Release Your Power" or "Let Your Presence Fall"
  • 01Intro. Morayo plays the melody line to cue Dr Caster in.
  • 02Verse one, solo. Dr Caster sings it alone. The band keeps time on hi-hat or ride only. Nothing else.
  • 03Verse two. Esther and the band come in.
  • 04Chorus. Can run up to four times, depending on where the room is.
  • 05Straight into Reign Jesus Reign. No stop.
Per part
  • Morayo: chords through the verse. There is no keyboard, so the guitar is carrying it. Overdrive off for the verse; it does not suit the dynamic.
  • Laolu: hi-hat or ride only under the solo verse. Time, not fill.
  • Dr Caster: verse one on your own. Wait for the melody cue.
Reign Jesus Reign A♭
Comes out of the chorus above with no break
  • 01Through once, solo. Dr Caster takes the whole song alone before anyone joins.
  • 02Band enters softly and lifts gradually from there.
  • 03The last two lines repeat. "King of Zion" and "Jesus reign".
  • 04Watch the fourth time round. The lyric changes. Do not sing "and this is holy ground" on that pass.
Per part
  • Everyone: the entry is soft. The intensity is built, not switched on.
  • Dr Caster: which section loops, and how many times, is yours to set.
Bow Down and Worship A♭
The transition into this one takes care, because the ear expects a different chord
  • 01Interlude. Morayo plays the melody line, not chords. This keeps time and gives Dr Caster a clean cue without the key shift confusing the entry.
  • 02Verse twice, solo. Dr Caster alone.
  • 03Third time, everyone joins. Band and Esther.
  • 04Fourth time to finish the verse section, then into the chorus.
Per part
  • Morayo: melody line for the interlude. Chords were tried and they muddied the transition.
  • Everyone: four verses in total. Two solo, two together. Count them.

BJ ran the structure back at the end by asking the team to say it out loud rather than assuming it had landed. That is a good habit and worth keeping.

04 Offering & Close · A♭

Two simple ones
to finish.

Both stay in A♭, so there is no key change to manage after worship.

Offering: Higher Higher. The "every day I live for Jesus" song from the medley we did recently. Straightforward, and everyone already has it.

End of service: You Are Worthy. "King of kings, Lord of lords, You are worthy." An old one that Calvary has sung for years. It needs no arrangement, only a clean start.

Keeping both in A♭ was a deliberate call. Praise sits in B and everything after it sits a semitone below, so the only move in the whole service is the one drop into worship.
