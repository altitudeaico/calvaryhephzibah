He Never Fails

Suggested route for a 45–60 minute group session

All three sessions can be covered in one meeting. Open together (5 min) → work through all three "Look at the passage" and "Talk it through" sections (roughly 12 minutes each, ~36 min total — you don't need every question, pick what fits your group and time) → close with one shared application and prayer (10 min). If you're short on time, sessions 1 and 2 sit together naturally since they're one continuous story.

Three short reflections for this week — one for today, and one for each of the next two days you set aside, or all three together if you're meeting as a group. Each one opens by reading a passage yourself, before any explanation. Based on Sunday's message, but not a transcript of it.

1. When You Feel Afraid

Exodus 14:10–15
Exodus 14:10–14 (ESV)
"As Pharaoh drew near, the people of Israel lifted up their eyes, and behold, the Egyptians were marching after them, and they feared greatly... They said to Moses, 'Is it because there were no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness?'... Moses said to the people, 'Fear not, stand firm, and see the salvation of the LORD... The LORD will fight for you, and you have only to be silent.'"

Look at the passage

  • What happens in this passage? What do the people do, and what does Moses say back to them?

Understanding the passage

What's happening: Israel is trapped — sea ahead, Egypt's army closing in behind, no visible way out. They panic and turn on Moses.

What we learn about God: He doesn't panic when His people do. He gives a concrete instruction, not just comfort — "stand firm" is something to do, not just a feeling to have.

How this connects with the sermon: this is the passage the message builds on — the instruction to trust comes before any evidence that things will work out.

One practical implication: fear doesn't have to be resolved before you can act in trust. Standing firm and being afraid can happen at the same time.

Talk it through

  • What's one thing you're facing right now where you can't yet see a way through?
  • What would you like to understand better?

Try this

Name one concern honestly — out loud or written down — and bring it to God once this week.

Leading this session

  • Timing: ~12 minutes if run alone; less if combined with session 2.
  • Possible answers to the observe question: the people despair and blame Moses for bringing them out to die; Moses tells them not to be afraid, to stand firm, and that the Lord will fight for them.
  • Welcoming someone new to the Bible: the observe question is designed to be answerable just by reading — lean on it first if the group includes newcomers.
  • If one person is dominating: "let's hear a couple of other people on that one" works better than cutting someone off directly.

2. Remembering God's Faithfulness

Exodus 14:30–15:2
What happened in between Between Moses' words in session one (14:13–14) and the song below, the sea itself is the story: God tells Moses to stretch out his hand, the sea splits and Israel crosses on dry ground, and when the pursuing Egyptian army follows them in, the waters return and the threat is gone entirely — not just avoided. The full account is Exodus 14:15–31; worth reading in full if you have time this week.
Exodus 14:30–15:1 (ESV)
"Thus the LORD saved Israel that day from the hand of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore. Israel saw the great power that the LORD used against the Egyptians, so the people feared the LORD, and they believed in the LORD... Then Moses and the people of Israel sang this song to the LORD..."
Worth knowing: "feared the LORD" here doesn't mean being afraid of Him the way Israel was afraid of Pharaoh's army. It means taking God seriously — recognising Him as real and worth honouring, which is why the very next thing they do is trust and sing, not run.

Look at the passage

  • What does the song in 15:1–2 say about who God is, not just what He did?

Understanding the passage

What's happening: Israel sees the outcome fully — the threat isn't just past, it's ended — and their fear turns into open worship.

What we learn about God: He follows through completely. He's not just described as a rescuer here; He's sung about as one.

How this connects with the sermon: the shift from fear (session 1) to singing, in one chapter, is the pattern the message centres on.

One practical implication: remembering what God has already done is itself a form of worship, not just a coping technique.

Talk it through

  • Is there something in your own life you could genuinely sing about, even briefly, from something God has already brought you through?
  • What would you like to understand better?

Try this

Write down one thing you still thank God for. Sharing it with a group is optional.

Leading this session

  • Timing: ~12 minutes, or shorter combined with session 1.
  • Possible answer to the observe question: the song calls God strength, salvation, and describes His right hand as glorious in power — the emphasis is on who He is, not only what happened.
  • Watch for the gap between "God was faithful then" and "so God owes me the same outcome now" — the passage doesn't say the second thing. If it comes up, it's worth naming gently.

3. Praying While You Wait

Philippians 4:4–7
Philippians 4:4–7 (ESV)
"Rejoice in the Lord always; again I will say, rejoice. Let your reasonableness be known to everyone. The Lord is at hand; do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus."
Worth knowing: "supplication" simply means asking God earnestly for help — it's not a formal or old-fashioned category, just an honest, specific request.

Sunday's message referenced verse 4 — "rejoice always" — written by Paul from prison. This session reads the fuller passage around it.

Look at the passage

  • What does Paul actually tell the Philippians to do with their anxiety, and what does he say the result is?

Understanding the passage

What's happening: Paul, writing from prison, tells a church he loves to rejoice, to stop being anxious, and to bring their requests to God alongside genuine thanks.

What we learn about God: He welcomes ongoing, honest requests — not silence, and not pretending everything's fine. And He offers peace that guards the heart during the waiting, not only after the answer arrives.

How this connects with the sermon: this is the "rejoice always" passage the message referenced directly.

One practical implication: request and thanksgiving can sit in the same prayer, at the same time.

Talk it through

  • What might it look like to bring an honest request to God this week, along with genuine thanks, in the same prayer?
  • What would you like to understand better?

Try this

Bring one honest request and one genuine thanks to God, in the same prayer, once this week.

Leading this session

  • Timing: ~12 minutes, or shorter combined with the others.
  • Possible answer to the observe question: pray about everything with thanksgiving rather than being anxious; the result is God's peace guarding heart and mind, not a guarantee of a specific outcome.
  • This session is the most likely to surface real, ongoing pain. Don't ask people to name their specific request aloud unless they offer to — the application is meant to stay private by default.

Pray this, or pray your own version

Lord, thank You for what You've already brought me through. Teach me to bring what I'm carrying now honestly, with thanks alongside it, even before I can see how it resolves. Guard my heart while I wait. Amen.

Responding when someone shares real pain

If someone raises unanswered prayer or a hard situation during discussion:

  • Acknowledge what they shared specifically — repeat back the shape of it, don't rush past it.
  • Ask if they'd welcome prayer right there, or would rather talk privately after.
  • You don't need to solve it or explain why it's happened. Being heard is the goal of the moment, not resolution.
  • Follow up with them personally afterwards if they seemed to want more space than the group setting allowed.
